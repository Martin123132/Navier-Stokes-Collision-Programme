# Compatible eight-cell cubic pressure graph

Status: exact finite-dimensional reduction of the globally compatible
eight-cell terminal-weight problem. Coefficient compatibility can annihilate
real pressure edges, but compatibility alone gives no uniform strict gain
over the sharp directionwise envelope. The remaining question is the load
cone generated by divergence-free velocity-pressure pairs. No critical
estimate or Navier-Stokes regularity conclusion is claimed.

## 1. The compatible coefficient space

Let

```text
phi_s(x)=(1+s cos(mx))/2,  s in {-1,+1},

Phi_v(x)=product_(j=1)^3 phi_(v_j)(x_j),

lambda_w=sum_(v in {-1,+1}^3) w_v Phi_v,  w_v>=0.
```

For direction `j`, interpolate the two opposite coefficient faces:

```text
A_j(x_hat_j)
 =sum_(v_hat_j) w_(+,v_hat_j) Phi_(v_hat_j),

B_j(x_hat_j)
 =sum_(v_hat_j) w_(-,v_hat_j) Phi_(v_hat_j),

H_j=A_j+B_j,
D_j=A_j-B_j.
```

If

```text
e_j(x_hat_j)
 =mean_(x_j)[p u_j partial_j phi_+(mx_j)],
```

then the exact pressure/Fisher objective is

```text
S_e(w)=L_e(w)-c Q(w),                               (1.1)

c=nu m^2/16,

L_e(w)=sum_j mean_(x_hat_j)[D_j e_j],

Q(w)=sum_j mean_(x_hat_j)[H_j D_j^2].              (1.2)
```

Unlike the directionwise relaxation, all twelve conditional edges in (1.2)
are generated by the same eight coefficients.

## 2. Exact pressure-load projection

The pressure term is linear in the coefficient vector:

```text
L_e(w)=b(e).w,                                      (2.1)

b_v
 =sum_j v_j mean_(x_hat_j)[Phi_(v_hat_j)e_j].       (2.2)
```

Summing (2.2) over all vertices gives

```text
sum_v b_v=0.                                        (2.3)
```

Thus a common coefficient shift is invisible to pressure. More importantly,
each `D_j` lies in the four-dimensional tensor span

```text
span{1,cos(mx_k)}
 tensor
span{1,cos(mx_l)},                                  (2.4)
```

where `{j,k,l}={1,2,3}`. Only the projection of `e_j` onto (2.4) contributes.
This projection must be taken before an `L^(3/2)` absolute-value estimate.

The exact one-dimensional partition moments used to evaluate `Q` are

```text
mean[phi_a phi_b phi_c]
 =5/16  if a=b=c,
 =1/16  otherwise.                                 (2.5)
```

Equations (1.2) and (2.5) give an explicit rational cubic polynomial in the
eight entries of `w`.

## 3. Projective reduction

For a fixed nonnegative ray `w=t z`, put

```text
B=b.z,
Q=Q(z).
```

If `B>0`, exact scalar optimization gives

```text
sup_(t>=0)[tB-c t^3Q]
 =2 B^(3/2)/(3sqrt(3cQ)).                           (3.1)
```

The quotient in (3.1) is invariant under scaling `z`. Moreover,

```text
Q(w+a1)=Q(w)+2a R(w),                               (3.2)

R(w)=sum_j mean D_j^2.
```

Because `b.1=0`, subtracting a positive common floor preserves the pressure
load and decreases the penalty. The global search may therefore be
restricted to

```text
z>=0,
min_v z_v=0.                                        (3.3)
```

Also, `Q(z)=0` for a nonnegative vector exactly when `z` is constant. Hence
(3.1)-(3.3) are a finite, attained projective variational problem.

## 4. The cubic energy is not convex

The phrase "convex cubic graph dual" is incorrect here. An exact witness is

```text
w=(1,2,3,4,2,3,4,1),

h=(1/2,1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4,1/2).
```

All entries of `w-h`, `w`, and `w+h` are nonnegative, while

```text
Q(w-h)=24391/1024,

Q(w)=95/4,

Q(w+h)=23625/1024.
```

Consequently

```text
Q(w)
 -(Q(w-h)+Q(w+h))/2
 =39/128>0.                                         (4.1)
```

This is an exact violation of convexity. Generic local optimization cannot
certify the global supremum without an independent bound.

## 5. Compatibility alone gives no uniform gain

Fix a vertex `v` and a scale `t>0`. Choose

```text
w=t delta_v.
```

Then for every direction

```text
D_j=v_j t Phi_(v_hat_j),

H_j=t Phi_(v_hat_j).                                (5.1)
```

For fixed `c`, define the smooth abstract edge flux

```text
e_j
 =3c v_j t^2 Phi_(v_hat_j)^2.                       (5.2)
```

Equations (5.1)-(5.2) satisfy the pointwise scalar optimizer relation

```text
sign(e_j)=sign(D_j),

|e_j|=3c |D_j|^2,

H_j=|D_j|.                                          (5.3)
```

Since

```text
mean phi_s^3=5/16,

mean Phi_(v_hat_j)^3=25/256,
```

the normalized values `c=t=1` are

```text
Q(w)=75/256,

L_e(w)=225/256,

S_e(w)=75/128.                                      (5.4)
```

The sum of the three sharp directionwise envelopes is also `75/128`.
Because the directionwise bound applies to every compatible coefficient
vector and (5.1) attains it, (5.4) is the global compatible supremum.

The corresponding normalized zero-sum load vector has a simple exact
profile. If a load vertex lies at Hamming distance `d` from the selected
vertex `v`, its entry is

```text
d=0:  225/256,

d=1:  -45/256,

d=2:  -27/256,

d=3:   -9/256.                                      (5.5)
```

The multiplicities are `1,3,3,1`, so these entries sum to zero. The eight
cube translates of (5.5) are the precise rays for the next realizability
test.

Therefore there is no universal `kappa<1` such that

```text
compatible graph supremum
 <=kappa times directionwise envelope               (5.6)
```

for all smooth conditional edge-flux functions.

This is not yet a Navier-Stokes counterexample. The flux family (5.2) has
not been realized as

```text
mean_(x_j)[p u_j partial_j phi_+]
```

for a divergence-free velocity and its induced pressure.

## 6. Taylor-Green shows the opposite extreme

For

```text
u=(sin x cos y,-cos x sin y,0),

p=(cos 2x+cos 2y)/4,
```

and partition frequency one, the conditional pressure edges simplify to

```text
e_x(y)=-cos(3y)/32,

e_y(x)= cos(3x)/32,

e_z=0.                                              (6.1)
```

Every mode in (6.1) is orthogonal to the compatible load space (2.4).
Hence

```text
b_v=0 for all eight vertices,

sup_(w>=0) S_e(w)=0.                                (6.2)
```

The directionwise `L^(3/2)` envelope is nevertheless strictly positive.
Thus graph compatibility can give a complete gain on an actual
Navier-Stokes pressure field, even though Section 5 rules out a gain based
on compatibility alone.

## 7. Route decision

Established:

- the full compatible objective depends only on a seven-dimensional
  zero-sum load vector;
- the nonnegative coefficient supremum has an exact projective reduction;
- its cubic energy is nonconvex;
- arbitrary smooth edge fluxes can saturate all relaxed directions at once;
- Taylor-Green is annihilated by the compatible load projection.

Still open:

- the range of load vectors generated by divergence-free velocity-pressure
  pairs;
- whether a vertex-saturating load ray is PDE-realizable or approximable;
- a scale-invariant separation if those rays are excluded;
- pressure absorption, low-regularity passage, and global regularity.

## 8. Next theorem target

Derive the Fourier-triad map

```text
divergence-free velocity coefficients
  -> pressure coefficients
  -> seven-dimensional load b.                      (8.1)
```

The first finite target is a bounded mode family containing every triad that
can reach conditional frequencies `0` or `m` after multiplication by
`partial_j phi_+`. Test all eight vertex-saturating load rays against the
algebraic image and its closure.

Two outcomes are useful:

1. A realized ray gives a PDE-level no-go for compatibility alone.
2. A proved exclusion gives a new pressure-structure inequality to quantify.
