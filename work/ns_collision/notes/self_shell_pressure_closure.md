# Full self-shell pressure closure

Status: the actual, uncut pressure generated by one annular velocity shell
is controlled in its single-vertex local energy load. The proof combines
exact far-low Fourier orthogonality with the annular high-output commutator
theorem. Constants are gap-dependent for `K>sqrt(3)m` and uniform when
`K>=2sqrt(3)m`. Cross-shell high-high-to-low pressure remains open. No
Navier-Stokes regularity conclusion is claimed.

## 1. The question left by the annular theorem

Let `u_K` be smooth, periodic, divergence free, and supported in

```text
K<=|k|<=Lambda K.                                  (1.1)
```

At a tensor partition vertex `v`, let

```text
Phi_v(x)
 =product_(j=1)^3 [1+sigma_(v,j)cos(mx_j)]/2,

sigma_(v,j) in {-1,+1}.                            (1.2)
```

The preceding annular theorem controls

```text
mean[p_hi u_K dot grad Phi_v]                      (1.3)
```

when `p_hi` is obtained from `u_K tensor u_K` by a smooth double-Riesz
multiplier that vanishes near output frequency zero. The unresolved point
was whether the low output of

```text
p_K=R_iR_j(u_(K,i)u_(K,j))                        (1.4)
```

could contribute to the same-shell load.

It cannot, once the low cutoff lies below the spectral gap between the
velocity shell and the partition stencil.

## 2. Exact partition stencil

Every one-dimensional factor in (1.2) has Fourier support

```text
{0,-m,+m}.
```

Therefore

```text
supp Fourier(grad Phi_v)
 subset {-m,0,m}^3 minus {0}.                      (2.1)
```

In particular, every occupied stencil frequency `r` obeys

```text
|r|<=R,  R=sqrt(3)m.                               (2.2)
```

This bound is independent of the vertex signs.

## 3. Far-low orthogonality lemma

Let `p_lo` be any scalar trigonometric polynomial with

```text
supp phat_lo subset {|q|<Q},                       (3.1)
```

and let `u` have no Fourier modes below `K`. Expanding the load gives

```text
mean[p_lo u dot grad Phi_v]
 =sum_(q+k+r=0)
   phat_lo(q) uhat(k) dot Fourier(grad Phi_v)(r).  (3.2)
```

Every summand in (3.2) would require

```text
K<=|k|
 <=|q|+|r|
 <Q+R.                                             (3.3)
```

Consequently,

```text
Q+R<=K

implies

mean[p_lo u dot grad Phi_v]=0.                     (3.4)
```

The strict inequality in (3.1) permits equality in the first line of
(3.4). This lemma is purely spectral: it does not use the pressure equation
or incompressibility.

## 4. Adaptive smooth decomposition

Assume the same threshold already needed by the square-factor bridge,

```text
K>R=sqrt(3)m.                                      (4.1)
```

Define the relative gap

```text
delta=1-R/K>0.                                     (4.2)
```

Choose a fixed radial `C^3` cutoff `chi_0` such that

```text
chi_0(eta)=0  for |eta|<=1/4,

chi_0(eta)=1  for |eta|>=1/2.                      (4.3)
```

Set

```text
chi_(K,delta)(xi)=chi_0(xi/(delta K)),

p_hi=chi_(K,delta)(D)p_K,

p_lo=[1-chi_(K,delta)(D)]p_K.                      (4.4)
```

The low multiplier vanishes when

```text
|q|>=delta K/2=(K-R)/2.                            (4.5)
```

Thus its support satisfies

```text
|q|+|r|
 <(K-R)/2+R
 =(K+R)/2
 <K.                                               (4.6)
```

The far-low lemma now gives the exact identity

```text
mean[p_lo u_K dot grad Phi_v]=0,                   (4.7)

mean[p_K u_K dot grad Phi_v]
 =mean[p_hi u_K dot grad Phi_v].                   (4.8)
```

The cutoff is only a proof decomposition. Equation (4.8) concerns the
actual uncut pressure (1.4).

## 5. Smooth high multiplier

Define the matrix multiplier

```text
M_(K,delta,ij)(xi)
 =chi_0(xi/(delta K)) xi_i xi_j/|xi|^2,            (5.1)
```

with value zero near `xi=0`. It is globally `C^3`. Since its transition
occurs at scale `delta K`,

```text
sup_xi ||partial_S M_(K,delta)(xi)||
 <=D_|S| delta^(-|S|)K^(-|S|),  |S|<=3,           (5.2)
```

where `D_0,...,D_3` depend only on the fixed cutoff and dimension.

For coordinate degree

```text
L=floor(Lambda K),
```

the sharp residue-chain theorem gives

```text
C_(L,m)
 =cot(pi/[2(ceil((2L+1)/m)+1)]),                  (5.3)

theta_K=(m/(2K))C_(L,m)
 <=2(Lambda+1)/pi.                                (5.4)
```

Insert (5.2)-(5.4) into the exact eight-shift Walsh identity. All Hamming
leakage collapses back to vertex `v` with constant

```text
C_full
 =D_0
  +3D_1 delta^(-1)theta_K
  +3D_2 delta^(-2)theta_K^2
  +D_3 delta^(-3)theta_K^3.                       (5.5)
```

Hence

```text
||psi_v p_hi||_2
 <=C_full||u_K||_infinity||psi_v u_K||_2.          (5.6)
```

## 6. Full self-shell pressure theorem

Let

```text
E_v=mean[Phi_v|grad u_K|^2],

gamma(K,m)
 =1+sqrt(1+(3m^2/4)/[K(K-sqrt(3)m)]).             (6.1)
```

The square-factor bridge supplies

```text
||psi_v u_K||_2
 <=E_v^(1/2)/sqrt(K(K-sqrt(3)m)),                 (6.2)

||u_K grad psi_v||_2
 <=gamma(K,m)E_v^(1/2).                           (6.3)
```

Using `grad Phi_v=2psi_v grad psi_v`, followed by (4.8) and
(5.6), proves

```text
|mean[p_K u_K dot grad Phi_v]|

 <=[2 gamma C_full ||u_K||_infinity
    /sqrt(K(K-sqrt(3)m))]E_v.                     (6.4)
```

This is a bound for the complete pressure generated and tested by the same
annular shell. In particular, that pressure contribution is absorbed by
`nu E_v` whenever

```text
nu
 >=2 gamma C_full ||u_K||_infinity
   /sqrt(K(K-sqrt(3)m)).                           (6.5)
```

The constant in (6.4) deteriorates like a finite polynomial in
`delta^(-1)` as the shell carrier approaches the partition stencil.

## 7. Uniform strong-gap corollary

If

```text
K>=2sqrt(3)m,                                      (7.1)
```

one can avoid an adaptive cutoff. Choose a fixed smooth multiplier that is
zero for `|xi|<=K/4` and one for `|xi|>=K/2`. Its low complement is
supported in `|q|<K/2`, and

```text
|q|+|r|<K/2+sqrt(3)m<=K.                           (7.2)
```

The low load again vanishes. All derivative constants are now uniform in
`K/m`, and (6.4) holds with a constant depending only on `Lambda`, the
fixed cutoff, and dimension.

The threshold (7.1) is a convenient uniform condition, not a claim of
sharpness.

## 8. Sparse adversarial replay

The audit constructs six exact finite-Fourier, divergence-free fields:
three phase choices at `(K,m)=(4,1)` and their co-scaled copies at
`(K,m)=(8,2)`. Every field lies in `K<=|k|<=2K`.

These fields were designed to contain:

- nearly opposite mode pairs generating genuine pressure below `K/2`;
- nonzero high-output pressure work against the vertex stencil;
- no possible low-output/testing-shell/stencil triple.

Across all six rows:

```text
minimum smooth-low pressure L2       0.289934611454

minimum full pressure load           0.010299103784

maximum full pressure load           0.042595885754

maximum smooth-low pressure load     0

maximum full-minus-high residual     0

maximum divergence residual          1.84e-15.      (8.1)
```

Thus the vanishing load is not caused by absent low pressure or a
pressure-free field.

A separate exact field at `(K,m)=(3,1)` has a nonzero `K/2`-low load
`2.498884741688e-6`. This lies below the uniform condition (7.1), showing
that the fixed half-carrier split cannot simply be reused there. It does
not contradict the adaptive theorem: the adaptive cutoff moves that mode
into `p_hi`.

## 9. What is and is not closed

Established:

- exact far-low pressure-load orthogonality;
- a gap-dependent full self-shell theorem for every `K>sqrt(3)m`;
- a uniform fixed-cutoff corollary for `K>=2sqrt(3)m`;
- control of the actual pressure without requiring a sharp multiplier
  estimate;
- an explicit intrinsic absorption condition for this complete self-shell
  pressure component;
- adversarial finite-mode replay with genuine low pressure and total work.

Not established:

- pressure generated by high shells and tested against a different low
  shell;
- comparable-shell, separated-shell, or mixed paraproduct summation;
- a signed critical bound for the complete nonlinear flux;
- low-regularity passage, exceptional-set removal, or global regularity.

## 10. Next obstruction

The low-output high-high beat is not a self-shell obstruction. It becomes
genuine in a three-shell interaction:

```text
p_L=P_L R_iR_j(u_(H,i)u_(H,j)),

mean[p_L u_L dot grad Phi_v],

H>>L>=m.                                           (10.1)
```

The next stage should take a slowly modulated divergence-free oscillation
at carrier `H`, derive its limiting low Reynolds stress and induced
pressure as `H` tends to infinity, and determine whether (10.1) has any
decay in `H`.

If pressure alone has no such gain, the next object is the signed sum of
pressure work and kinetic transport work. Those terms belong to the same
local energy flux and must be combined before taking absolute values.
