# Heat-Scale Cubic Cancellation

Date: 2026-07-17

Status: exact identities for smooth divergence-free fields and a tested
critical-norm obstruction. No unconditional Navier-Stokes regularity estimate
is claimed.

## 1. Collision-Scale Split

Let

```text
s=2*nu*tau,
S_s=exp(s*Delta)S,
B_s=S-S_s.
```

Here `S_s` is generated by the regularized kernel from
`two_point_vorticity_collision_system.md`, with collision length
`L=2*sqrt(s)=sqrt(8*nu*tau)`. In Fourier variables,

```text
S_s_hat(xi)=exp(-s*|xi|^2)S_hat(xi),
B_s_hat(xi)=[1-exp(-s*|xi|^2)]S_hat(xi).          (1.1)
```

Thus `S_s` is the collision-regularized low-frequency strain and `B_s` is
the near-collision high-frequency defect.

For divergence-free vorticity, the strain multiplier is an exact scaled
isometry:

```text
||S||_2^2=(1/2)||omega||_2^2.                    (1.2)
```

Consequently,

```text
||B_s||_2^2
 =(1/2) integral [1-exp(-s*|xi|^2)]^2
                 |omega_hat(xi)|^2 dxi.          (1.3)
```

Equation (1.3) proves a sharp energy-level obstruction. Although `B_s` tends
to zero for each fixed `omega` as `s` tends to zero, there is no uniform rate
on the `L^2` unit ball. Vorticity concentrated near `|xi|=s^(-1/2)` retains
the fixed fraction

```text
(1-exp(-1))/sqrt(2)
```

of its `L^2` norm in `B_s`. Any smallness estimate

```text
||B_s||_2 <= o(1)||omega||_2
```

is therefore false without additional information.

More generally, for `0<=alpha<=2`,

```text
||B_s||_2
 <=(s^(alpha/2)/sqrt(2))||omega||_(Hdot^alpha).  (1.4)
```

Every positive power of the collision scale costs a positive derivative of
vorticity, which is not supplied by the Leray energy inequality.

## 2. Tensor Cancellation Survives the Split

The norm obstruction does not see the cubic contraction. For local
vorticity `a=omega(x)` and remote vorticity `b=omega(x-r)`, the strain kernel
is linear in `b` and obeys

```text
a dot K(r)[a] a=0.                                (2.1)
```

Since both heat multipliers `M` and `1-M` are nonnegative radial scalars,
the same cancellation holds separately for `S_s` and `B_s`. In particular,

```text
a dot B_s(r)[b] a
 =a dot B_s(r)[b-a] a.                            (2.2)
```

The sharper direction form subtracts the vector parallel to `a` and bounds
the positive defect by a weighted vorticity-direction increment. This is the
geometric depletion mechanism behind filtered near-field estimates. The
collision split therefore preserves, rather than averages away, the angular
cancellation needed to use it.

## 3. Vanishing First Heat-Scale Moment

Define the global defect stretching functional

```text
D(s)=<S-S_s,omega tensor omega>.                  (3.1)
```

A theorem of Miller gives, for every sufficiently smooth divergence-free
field,

```text
<-Delta S,omega tensor omega>=0.                  (3.2)
```

Therefore

```text
D(0)=0,
D'(s)=<-Delta S_s,omega tensor omega>,
D'(0)=0.                                          (3.3)
```

The collision-boundary defect has a double zero at heat scale zero. More
precisely,

```text
D''(0)=-<Delta^2 S,omega tensor omega>,

D(s)=-(s^2/2)<Delta^2 S,omega tensor omega>
     +o(s^2).                                     (3.4)
```

This cancellation is invisible in the operator norm (1.3). It says that the
leading high-frequency boundary defect is globally orthogonal to the exact
vorticity pair that generates it.

## 4. Polarized Identity

For divergence-free fields `a,b,c`, define the trilinear form

```text
T(a;b,c)=<-Delta S(a),
          sym[omega(b) tensor omega(c)]>.
```

It is symmetric in `b,c`. Polarizing `T(v;v,v)=0` gives

```text
T(b;a,a)+2T(a;a,b)=0.                             (4.1)
```

Taking `b=exp(s*Delta)a` rewrites the scale derivative as

```text
D'(s)
 =-2T(a;exp(s*Delta)a-a,a).                       (4.2)
```

Thus the first surviving boundary contribution contains a heat increment of
the vorticity, not merely the singular strain operator. This is the strongest
current non-circular structural gain.

## 5. Exact Pair-Determinant Formula

Put

```text
q=curl omega=-Delta u.
```

The vector identity used to prove (3.2) also gives, at every positive heat
scale,

```text
D'(s)=-integral q_s dot (q cross omega) dx,
q_s=exp(s*Delta)q.                                (5.1)
```

Writing the heat semigroup with its Gaussian kernel `G_s` and symmetrizing
the two spatial variables gives the exact two-copy formula

```text
D'(s)
 =-(1/2) double_integral G_s(x-y)
    det(q(y),q(x),omega(x)-omega(y)) dx dy.       (5.2)
```

This is a literal collision-diagonal cancellation. The oriented volume in
(5.2) vanishes when `x=y`, and the Gaussian pair separation is exactly the
relative heat law generated by independent viscous replicas. The first
surviving boundary term measures cross-scale twisting of `curl omega` and a
vorticity increment.

There is no universal sign. On the three-dimensional torus, the explicit
divergence-free triad

```text
u=(0,-1,-1)cos(x)
 +(-1,0,-1)cos(y)
 +(1,-1,1)sin(x+y)
```

satisfies

```text
D(s)=(1/2)[1-exp(-s)]^2>0.                        (5.3)
```

Replacing `u` by `-u` reverses the sign because `D` is cubic. Hence the
double zero is exact but cannot be upgraded to positivity without using the
Navier-Stokes dynamics or an additional rigidity property.

## 6. Relation to Finite-Scale Vorticity Work

The recent preprint
`references/yu_filtered_vortex/filtered_vortex_stretching_submission_ready.tex`
proves that positive singular near-field stretching of a spatially filtered
vorticity can be absorbed by filtered diffusion up to an enstrophy reservoir.
Its energy-class smoothing loss is the fifth power of the ratio between
physical and filter scales. Independently, our exact Gaussian collision
multiplier has

```text
M(z)=8*z^5/(15*sqrt(pi))+O(z^7).                  (6.1)
```

The matching fifth power is not a closure theorem, but it identifies the
same critical scale in two formulations:

- stochastic relative heat flow supplies the canonical Gaussian filter;
- the exact multiplier supplies its radial collision law;
- angular cancellation converts the remaining singular defect to increments;
- diffusion pays the local increment, while far-field, commutator, and
  localization terms remain.

The cited finite-scale theorem is explicitly conditional at the final
unweighted closure stage. Its surviving gates are far-field affine-strain
jets, scale-critical velocity-increment defects, and localization budgets.

## 7. Collision-Rigidity Continuation Criterion

The split gives a clean conditional theorem without filtering the vorticity
equation. Let

```text
E(t)=||omega(t)||_2^2,
Q(t)=||grad omega(t)||_2^2=||q(t)||_2^2,
U_0=||u_0||_2.
```

For a smooth unforced solution on `R^3`, the energy inequality and the heat
kernel estimate give

```text
||S_s(t)||_infinity
 <=C_0*s^(-5/4)U_0,

C_0=sqrt(3)/[2*(8*pi)^(3/4)].                    (7.1)
```

Indeed, `S_s=sym grad exp(s*Delta)u` and

```text
||grad G_s||_2=C_0*s^(-5/4).
```

The enstrophy identity therefore becomes

```text
(1/2)E'(t)+nu*Q(t)
 =<S_s,omega tensor omega>+D(s)

 <=C_0*s^(-5/4)U_0*E(t)+D(s).                   (7.2)
```

Fix any `s>0`. If there are `theta<1` and a nonnegative
`a in L^1(0,T)` such that

```text
D(s,t)<=theta*nu*Q(t)+a(t)E(t)                  (7.3)
```

for almost every time before a putative singular time, then

```text
E(t)<=E(0)exp(
  2*C_0*s^(-5/4)U_0*t+2*integral_0^t a(r)dr).   (7.4)
```

Thus the smooth solution continues past `T`. In particular, the sufficient
condition

```text
[D(s,t)]_+/(nu*Q(t))<=theta<1                   (7.5)
```

is a collision-rigidity regularity criterion. By (5.2), its numerator is
exactly

```text
[-(1/2) integral_0^s double_integral G_r(x-y)
  det(q(y),q(x),omega(x)-omega(y)) dx dy dr]_+. (7.6)
```

This criterion is not the desired unconditional estimate. Its value is that
all low-frequency and far-field strain is already paid by kinetic energy,
with no commutator term, while the remaining obstruction is one signed,
dimensionless two-copy quantity. A proof now needs to derive (7.3) from the
Navier-Stokes dynamics rather than assume it.

## 8. Next Proof Gate

The next useful target is not a raw norm estimate for `B_s`; (1.3) rules that
out. It is a scale-integrated estimate that retains both cancellations:

```text
1. pointwise angular cancellation from (2.2);
2. global first-moment cancellation from (3.3)-(4.2);
3. pair-diagonal oriented-volume cancellation from (5.2).
```

A successful estimate must show that the polarized heat increment in (4.2),
possibly averaged over the independent-replica collision scale, is paid by
Navier-Stokes dissipation without assuming a critical continuation norm. If
this fails, the failure should be represented as an explicit recurrent
cross-scale defect rather than hidden in an absolute-value estimate.
